0.1.3:

- No fuzz and no screen filters to start. The fine grey fuzz over everything lit was a second film grain the game
  draws every frame, apart from its Noise Effect, so turning Film grain off never took it away. Both are gone now
  unless you turn "Film grain" on (Options, View), and the picture keeps its brightness (the darkest parts a shade
  darker, as the grain's specks no longer lift them). Drifting fog, sharpening, Smooth edges and the comfort vignette
  are off to start too.
- Smooth turning is the default (snap 45 degrees: Options, Comfort). It now turns at its set speed even when the game
  runs slower than the headset.
- Physical melee: moving your arm gently no longer swings, with any weapon or your fists (knife, steel pipe, katana,
  maul, stun gun, a gun as a club, punches). A swing's speed is now measured in real time (at the game's 50 to 60
  frames a second on a 120 Hz headset, hands were read as moving twice as fast as they did); a swing needs more
  speed and a longer path than before, and has to stay fast for a moment. Real swings hit as before.
- Heather's shadow is softer: half as dark where only she shades the beam, no darker where the room's own shadow
  already is, and none from her hand right at the flashlight's lens.
- Updating from 0.1.2: your options take 0.1.3's new defaults once (the ones above, and the picture at 100%); your
  saves are kept, and anything you change afterwards stays.
- "Cutscenes" in the VR menu (View) reads "Dynamic (her eyes or the camera)", the default. "At the camera" keeps
  every scene at the game's camera. 0.1.2's separate "Dynamic cutscenes" switch is part of this row now (your choice
  kept). Every VR option is in the VR menu (hold the left menu button for a second) and on the Options tab, not in
  the game's own menus.
- Quitting the game no longer ends in a crash: the logs are saved and the game closes cleanly.
- Dynamic resolution takes its step back when drawing smaller does not make the frames quicker (it only blurred the
  picture before).
- "Smoother frames (AppSW)" is gone from the options: Silent Hill 3 never gave the headset the motion it needs for it,
  so it did nothing. It comes back once it works.

0.1.2:

- The fog: a player saw a weird overlay like fog in front of their eyes. It was the game's own drifting fog, which
  VR drew as patches close in front of your face. It now hangs far off in the world, the way the PC VR mod draws it,
  and stays put as you turn your head. "Drifting fog" (Options, View) leaves it out altogether.
- A lamp's lens flare now sits at the lamp, no longer close in front of your face.
- Hold the left menu button for a second for the VR menu, anywhere: in play the game pauses under it. A short press
  closes it, and another goes back to play. Opening and closing it make a soft sound. First or third person is now
  the VR menu's "First person" switch (View).
- The picture, as the PC VR mod has it: Brightness, Contrast, Color saturation and Darker edges (at first the game's
  own look), and Smooth edges, on (Options, View, or the VR menu).
- Letting go of a two-hand grip: the other hand no longer jumps for a frame.
- Fewer log lines while blocking or reaching, so a long session writes much less.

0.1.1, a patch for 0.1.0:

- Manual reload, the shotgun: its shells now go into its loading port where you see it, under the receiver just in
  front of the trigger guard. In 0.1.0 the port was taken about 5 cm below the gun, in the air, so a shell pushed
  into the real port could count as loaded before it got there and was drawn going in under the gun.
- Every gun's reload by hand checked again on this build (handgun, shotgun, submachine gun, stun gun): magazine or
  shells in, the slide, pump or knob worked, the game's own counts, nothing free.
- The game's log now says where your hand put a magazine or shell against the gun's well for the first reloads, so a
  report shows how close a reload came if one ever does not take.

0.1.0, the first release: Silent Hill 3 in VR on the Quest, standalone, from your own PC copy of the game.

- First person at Heather's eyes, head and hands tracked (6DoF); walk in your room and she walks. Third person with the
  game's own camera whenever you like: hold the left menu button for a second.
- Cutscenes play around you, in the scene at the game's camera (or first person, or a cinema screen: Options, View).
- The game's movies play: the opening, the endings and the others, on a big cinema screen in front of you with their
  sound (they were skipped before). Skip one with the game's own button, as on the PC.
- Dynamic cutscenes: through Heather's eyes while the scene shows her, from the game's camera when it shows something
  else or her face up close. Off for steady third-person cutscenes: Options, View.
- Every weapon in your hand: aim where the gun points (a red guide dot shows it), hold long guns, the pipe, the katana
  and the maul with both hands.
- Physical melee: swing to hit, where the weapon really goes. Punch with empty hands (half the weakest weapon's hit);
  club with a gun.
- Holsters on your body: your gun over your right shoulder, your melee weapon on your right hip, each giving back the
  one you last put there; supplies in a belt pouch, the flashlight in your chest pocket.
- Spatial sound: every sound in the world comes from where it is around your head; turn your head and it stays put,
  and sounds behind you are softer. Options, View.
- Manual reload: drop the magazine (right stick down), take a new one from the ammo pouch at the front of your belt and
  push it into the gun; shells into the shotgun one at a time; then rack the handgun's slide, the submachine gun's
  knob or the shotgun's pump, and see them move. The game's own ammo and reload sounds, no free rounds; hints on the
  screen for your first reloads. Options, Hands and weapons.
- Heather's whole body follows yours: her own arms, hands and fingers go where your hands are, she leans, crouches,
  turns her head and steps as you do, and her body fades where it would fill your view. Her fingers stop on walls and
  objects; a ghost of her hand shows where yours is when it goes into a wall. Close your hand on an item or a door to
  take or use it.
- Her health and stamina on her watch: turn your left wrist toward you and look at it (health on top, pulsing as the
  game's own Life Display does when it runs low; stamina under it, draining as she runs). In first person the
  watch takes the place of the game's HUD; third person shows the game's normal HUD.
- The flashlight on her chest moves with her as you crouch or lean, and lights your hands and arms where they are in
  its beam, the side away from it dark.
- Her shadow: her whole body as you move it, her hands and fingers, and the weapon or the magazine you hold cast
  shadows in the flashlight's beam, in the game's own shadows (her raised hand darkens the beam on the floor and walls
  ahead; her legs' shadows fall at her feet). Turn it off or on any time: "Heather's shadow" on the VR menu's View
  page (or the launcher's Options, View).
- Block: hold your weapon or your forearm up between you and a monster and its hit does less damage.
- Shake to break free: when a monster knocks Heather down or grabs her, shake your controllers to get free sooner
  (the buttons still work too). Options, Hands and weapons.
- A sound for everything your hands do, heard from where they are: holsters, the pouch, the slide, the pump, a
  magazine, a hand on a wall, a gun knocked on one.
- A Silent Hill pointer for every screen: menus, the inventory, the map, memos, puzzles. Point at what you want and
  pull the trigger; your hand ticks and the dot grows on each thing a pull can choose, and a pull still works after
  holding the beam still while you read.
- Screens and messages made for VR: the inventory, the map, memos and menus open whole on a screen in front of you,
  fading in, and stay put while you point at them (turn far away and they come round to you); what Heather says
  about a thing shows right next to it, and subtitles sit low in your view. Options, View.
- The VR menu in the game (pause, then hold the left menu button) and the same options here, every VR feature on and
  the Quest 3's best performance options from the start.
- Both eyes are drawn together in one pass (each of the game's draws goes to both eyes at once), at 120 Hz with
  Smoother frames (AppSW) to start. A Quest 3 or 3S draws at the top resolution, and Dynamic resolution draws the
  game's 3D picture a little smaller for a moment whenever the headset starts falling behind (Options, Performance).
- Vibration for shots, hits, holsters, weapon changes, walls and floors under your hands, pickups and footsteps; a
  heartbeat at low health. Each kind can be turned off (Options, Vibration).
- Comfort: snap or smooth turning, a vignette, seated play, your height.
- The launcher copies the game in from a folder or a .zip, checks every file, brings in your PC saves, shows the logs
  and shares a report. Updates come from the project's GitHub releases.

Known for now: the extra weapons stay in Heather's own hand.
