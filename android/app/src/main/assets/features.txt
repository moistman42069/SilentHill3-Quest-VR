Dynamic cutscenes (on)
In a cutscene you see through Heather's eyes while the scene shows her, and from the game's camera when it shows
something else or her face up close. For steady third-person cutscenes, always from the game's camera: Options, View,
"Cutscenes", At the camera. The same row also offers first person all the way, or a cinema screen.

Your hands are hers
Her arms, hands and fingers follow yours. Her fingers stop on walls and objects; close your hand on an item, a handle
or a door to take or use it. Turn your left wrist toward you and look at her watch: her health and stamina show there.

Weapons
Grip at your right shoulder for your gun, at your right hip for your melee weapon; let go there to put it back. Supplies
are in the pouch at your belt, the flashlight in your chest pocket. Swing to hit; hold a weapon or your forearm up
between you and a monster to block. Reload by hand: right stick down drops the magazine, take a new one from the front
of your belt and push it in, then rack the slide. When a monster knocks you down or grabs you, shake your controllers to
get free.

Controls
Left stick walks (click it to run), right stick turns. The main trigger fires; melee weapons you swing. A is the action
button, X the map, Y the inventory, B the supplies. A short press of the left menu button pauses; hold it for a second
for the VR menu, anywhere (in play the game pauses under it). First or third person: the VR menu's View page, "First
person". The Controls tab has the whole list.

Comfort
Smooth turning (or snap), seated play and your height are on the Options tab, Comfort, with a vignette while
turning if you want one.

Picture and performance
120 Hz with Smoother frames (AppSW): the game makes up to 60 frames a second and gives the headset how the world
moved, and the headset makes the frame between each two itself, so walking and turning look smooth. Foveated
rendering at Medium, and on a Quest 3 or 3S the top resolution, with Dynamic resolution drawing the 3D picture a
little smaller for a moment if the headset starts falling behind (and back again if that does not help). Both
eyes are drawn together in one pass: each of the game's draws goes to both eyes at once, so the work of a frame is
done once.

The picture
No screen filters to start: no film grain (neither of the game's two), no drifting fog, no sharpening, no smoothing
and no vignette; the picture as the game draws it, and a lamp's lens flare at the lamp. Film grain, Drifting fog (far
off in the world, not in front of your eyes), Smooth edges, Brightness, Contrast, Color saturation and Darker edges
are on the Options tab, View, or in the VR menu in the game.

Every VR feature is on from the start, the screen filters off; each one can be changed on the Options tab or in
the VR menu in the game (hold the left menu button for a second).

Your copy of the game
Any PC copy of Silent Hill 3 (2003) should work: North American, European or Asian, CD or DVD, the exe as the disc
installed it (copy protection and all), a no-CD or no-DVD exe, or one changed by a fix such as the SH3 PC Fix. The
port runs its own copy of the game's code and only reads the exe's data, the same in every copy we know of. Not the
PS2 game or the PS3/Xbox 360 HD Collection. If the check is not sure, "Play in VR anyway" still tries it.
